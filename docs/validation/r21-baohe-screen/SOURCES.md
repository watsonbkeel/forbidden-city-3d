## Safe screen changes
- Replace a single uniformly gold flat slab/three rectangular crowns with three major framed vertical bays: wider central bay, narrower symmetric sides, raised curving central crest, and lower curved side shoulders. Include the small pale curved-top inset above the main central panel. Do not equate this with four equal panels.
- Source 1 supports warm pale beige/cream panel faces, rich red frames, narrow gilded edging, and more extensive gold on the curving crest. It does not establish the panel substrate, precise paint/metal formulation, carvings' exact motifs, or calibrated colors.
- Preserve a separate, smaller gilded throne centered in front. Source 2 shows a distinctly different/lower-looking seat/back arrangement; do not infer the exact current chair from either undated view or copy another hall's documented dimensions.
- A larger, plainer gold-toned rectangular backdrop is visible behind this shaped screen. Do not conflate its rectangle with the screen's silhouette or remove that background solely because the screen is being corrected.

Approximate manual image-space envelopes, with several-pixel tolerance; no perspective rectification. For source 1 only: the central framed bay is about 1.7–2.1 times either side bay's width; roughly 25% / 50% / 25% is a useful coarse allocation. Central gold crest width is roughly 55–65% of the full ornamented width; its top rises roughly 20–30% of total screen height above the side shoulders. The photographed throne occupies about 30–38% of the screen's width. These are broad visual constraints, not construction ratios.

## Fixed versus movable, and unknowns
The photos establish a distinct screen furnishing in front of the rear backdrop, not a solid gold wall. They do not show rear joinery, feet/fastenings, panel hinges, exact wing angles, thickness, or how it is attached. Classify it as a separate static furnishing in the scene; do not claim proven movability or architectural fixation. Lower central screen edges are partly hidden by the throne and objects; preserve conservative unseen construction. No exact meters, depth separation, current display date, original historic condition, or full carving reconstruction is established.

## Plaque in source 1
The large suspended plaque has a yellow/ochre field and black main lettering, surrounded by layered dull-gold/gray-gold edging and thin red bands. No blue field is visible. From image left to right, the four principal glyphs are “極 有 建 皇”; conventional right-to-left reading is “皇建有極”. The traditional character 極 is visible. Do not invent the small inscription/seal text.

Approximate unrectified outer-frame envelope: x452–756, y144–284; outer projected aspect about 2.15–2.25. Yellow letter field: x473–733, y161–262; aspect about 2.5–2.7. Source 2 does not show this plaque, so it cannot independently confirm the plaque's current presence or date. Its absence and the differing seat arrangement demonstrate why the two undated photographs must not be silently merged into a claim of a single verified-current display.
