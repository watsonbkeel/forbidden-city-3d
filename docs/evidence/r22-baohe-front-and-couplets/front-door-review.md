# Baohe front entrance: bounded evidence review

Reviewed 2026-10-18. Read-only source review; no implementation, tests, browser processes, or publication. Images were inspected at their native 1920-pixel width. The modern and historic photograph capture dates are not supplied; upload-path dates are not capture dates.

## Decision

**Use a four-leaf-width assembly for each of the central five front bays, not a six-leaf layout.** The modern photograph supports the middle three assemblies appearing as **outer two closed, inner two open**. The next assembly on each side has four closed full-height panels. This is strong evidence for the visible four-part composition, with a narrower qualification about operability: neither frontal photograph independently exposes every hinge or the concealed open leaves, so it does not prove all four parts' current operating mechanism or exact open-leaf angles.

## Evidence map

Number the nine facade bays 0–8 from photograph-left to photograph-right; central bay = 4. “Door” below describes a full-height enclosure reaching the threshold, contrasted with windows on a solid lower wall. This numbering is image-relative and does not assume a world-axis sign.

- Bays 2 and 6: full-height four-panel door assemblies; all four visibly closed in the modern photo. Lattice upper fields and solid lower panels continue down to the threshold. These are the outer two of the central five door bays, not window bays.
- Bays 3 and 5: full-height four-part door assemblies. One leaf-width panel remains face-on at each outer side; the central opening is approximately two such panel widths. Thus the photographed state supports two closed outer panels and two opened inner leaves. The opened leaves themselves are largely concealed in shadow/edge-on view.
- Bay 4: same four-part arrangement, at a wider bay scale. Modern photo shows a closed outer panel each side and a central opening approximately twice one panel's width. The throne is visible through this opening. A six-part model would require additional divisions or hidden parts for which these sources provide no positive evidence.
- Bays 0–1 and 7–8: window/enclosure zones on a visibly raised solid lower wall. The outermost zones are partly obscured by columns/perspective; this review does not establish their exact window-leaf count.

Native-photo audit locations, approximate pixel coordinates in the 1920 × 1440 modern image (visual locators, not dimensional measurements): bay 2 closed panels x≈505–632; bay 3 x≈687–818, opening x≈720–785; bay 4 x≈861–1048, opening x≈912–1003; bay 5 x≈1097–1229, opening x≈1130–1195; bay 6 closed panels x≈1278–1407. Inspect y≈686–831. Colonnade occlusion and perspective preclude extracting exact physical widths from these ranges.

## Independent checks and limits

- **Historic front photograph:** reproduces the central-five full-height versus outer-window distinction. The central bay appears to have only one inner leaf-width opening, with one panel to its left and two closed panels to its right. This independently supports four divisions and shows that the opening state is not fixed across photographs. Do not reproduce this historical open/closed state as the modern one.
- **1941 front elevation 49:** directly shows four full-height divisions in each of the central five door bays, and raised-wall window zones outside them. Its central and alternate door assemblies include a separately drawn upper crosspiece/frame detail; do not count crosspieces, lattice cells, or that overlay as extra door leaves. This is the clearest independent check against six leaves. It is a historical elevation, not proof of all current hinges or hardware.
- **1941 plan 48:** corroborates the front enclosure behind the south eave-column row and its distinction from the front colonnade. At the supplied resolution its tiny plan symbols do not independently resolve hinge counts or swing direction. It should not be used to claim more precise door mechanics.
- Modern photo shows railings directly across all three open viewing apertures, with additional foreground visitor barriers. A visually open door is therefore **not evidence of a permitted walk-through route**. Preserve any virtual access decision as a separate product choice rather than a factual claim about museum access.

Confidence: **high** for four-part rather than six-part visible composition, central-five door zones, modern middle-three open appearance, and raised-wall outer-window distinction; **moderate** for describing every outer full-height part as an independently movable leaf rather than a fixed/removable side part, because hinges are not exposed. **Unknown:** exact leaf dimensions/thickness, pivot/hinge construction, swing direction and resting angle of opened leaves, original-versus-restored joinery, current opening policy, and photo capture dates. No exact lattice motif/hardware reconstruction is established here.
