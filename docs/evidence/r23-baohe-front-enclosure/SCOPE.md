# R23: Baohe front enclosure candidate

The museum's front photograph and 1941 elevation show the central five full-height door bays and raised gray lower walls beneath windows in the outer four bays. The preceding candidate retained generic gold door surfaces throughout this facade. This scoped revision replaces those front-only surfaces with self-authored red frames, subdued lattice, dark blue-green recesses and gray lower walls; the rear facade and other halls are retained.

Sources:
- Official front photograph: https://file-pano.dpm.org.cn/hotspotImage/2025/08/19/dlV1Td6ERs6f20zINpkskcSWnuy6tdjhfEpVP0B9.jpg
- Front elevation, museum metadata 1941: https://file-pano.dpm.org.cn/hotspotImage/2026/06/25/5d9ce88cb6174d278ceeaa19da0eb1c8.jpg

Strong evidence supports the door/window distinction and broad palette. The solid lower wall looks roughly one quarter of enclosure height; 0.22 of the retained model door height is a provisional, slightly low visual estimate, not a measured height. Its0.30m thickness and small ledge projection are also provisional. The source does not establish stone versus another construction material, exact window leaf counts, operability, lattice motif, pigments, hinges or photograph capture date. Historical elevation is not proof of every current detail. No museum image is redistributed as a texture.

The four outer full-height boxes become lower solid bases plus shallower window infill. Two neighboring closed door bays remain solid and receive four-part drawn divisions. Twelve R22 open-assembly leaves retain exactly the same visible envelopes, hinges and physical colliders. All2487 registered colliders and120 walkables remain byte-identical; virtual access remains distinct from photographed visitor barriers.

Only wholly concealed bottom triangles are removed: bottom faces below the existing opaque floor, and window bottom faces covered by their wider solid bases. All sides, tops, shadows and navigation remain. This yields an actual net resident increment of4triangles,1material batch and384geometry-buffer bytes, including both LODs. It does not hide added geometry in another layer or raise the original performance threshold. Actual total camera budgets still require validation.

The private1024x512atlas uses standard geometric pattern interpretation. It is visually simplified and not a tracing, measured relief or a precise reconstruction of historic joinery. Lighting, exposure and global material response are unchanged.
