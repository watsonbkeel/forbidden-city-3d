# R48 NingShou nineteen-identity integration

The nineteen exact identities now select their own bounded profiles. This is
partial architecture implementation, not acceptance of complete museum interiors,
current inventory, visitor permission, or WebGL/native-device appearance.

The last six are Huangji, Yangxing, Leshou, Fuwang, Juanqin and Changyin. Their
floors, voids, enclosed portions and framing are real geometry, not floor-count
labels. Leshou's source gives ground-to-ridge 18.7 m; Changyin's 20.71 m datum is
explicitly interpreted as courtyard-to-top for the model because the short source
does not specify the measurement endpoints. All intermediate floor heights and
unmeasured dimensions remain fits. Upper visitor destinations are not added for
these six. Fitted Jingqi stairs retain their previously verified limited access.

## Existing Huangji layer

The unchanged aligned GLB contains the four golden columns, caisson and complete
throne group. The replacement profile does not construct duplicate golden columns
or another throne. Four original kit fallback meshes retain their original shape,
are isolated from material merging, and have independent owned materials. Only
these four are hidden after the actual asset validates and installs successfully;
failure, cancellation, unloading and suspension preserve or restore them. Other
merged golden objects are never hidden. The original conservative throne collider
fits inside the decoded GLB proxy within its documented submillimeter compression
error, with its footprint contained by the visible platform. It is not a detailed
carving collision model.

## Recovered names and source state

The generic old profiles registered model-name signs. The first replacement
profiles accidentally omitted these registrations. Nineteen original identity
texts now remain exactly once, including the retained Huangji sign. They are
explicitly legacy model identification, not verified historical plaque replicas.
The separately sourced Jingfu inner inscription is additional, not removed to
force an obsolete atlas count. Changyin's existing name is placed at the upper
level supported by its own source, with its setback and height fitted to the new
upper facade. The other two named inscriptions have not been newly fabricated.

## Consumer regressions and fixes

A newly raised, segmented offset-entrance route was wrongly rejected because the
old Huangji-to-NingShou side-route planner restarted every segment at courtyard
height. That bounded branch now carries the actual preceding segment's foot
height; the same physical collision checks remain. Blocking both registered side
portals still makes planning fail.

The two inherited cross-zone journeys terminate at the exterior central Huangji
viewpoint. Taking the first new lateral entry portal instead made their long
approach cut diagonally through a gate wall. They retain the central exterior
viewpoint, not a shortcut or a removed wall. After correction, all 63 automatic
routes pass their four real-controller frame-rate/sprint replays and baseline
check. All 63 R47 route names remain; 52 entire route objects are unchanged.
Eleven changed definitions have bounded architectural or neighboring-approach
reasons, including the Linxi approach around the fitted adjacent front buildings.

The old fixed atlas-count test is left unchanged. Its formerly masked current
four-cycle resource-ownership tail is tested separately, including one rendered
plane per registered name and exact owned-versus-borrowed disposal behavior.
Original failed run evidence remains distinguishable from the corrected reruns.
